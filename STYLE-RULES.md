# Anti-AI Writing Rules

The generic, reusable core of the system's style guide. Rationale: senior readers can spot LLM-generated copy; these rules are applied **at draft time**, not as a post-edit pass, and enforced by a self-check before any file is written.

## Punctuation

- **Em dash (—): never.** Replace with a period, semicolon, colon, comma, parentheses, or restructure. If a clause seems to need one, the sentence is doing too much work; split it.
- **En dash (–): numeric/date ranges only** ("Mar 2025 – Jun 2025"). Never as a sentence-level dash. Double-hyphen (`--`) equally banned. Ellipsis (…): avoid; restructure.

## Banned vocabulary

Cut unless a source document verbatim requires them: delve, navigate, leverage (verb), robust, transformative, seamless, pivotal, intricate, vibrant, tapestry, underscore, showcase, spearhead; "in today's fast-paced/digital/AI-first world"; "at the intersection of"; "unlock the value of / unlock potential"; "comprehensive understanding of"; "navigate the complexities of"; "drive measurable business outcomes"; "results-driven"; "passionate"; "highly motivated"; "team player"; "I am thrilled to / excited to / would love to".

## Sentence patterns to break

- "It's not X, it's Y" / "Not just X, but Y" — never.
- Triads / triadic parallelism: max one per 250 words.
- Stacked symmetric "X and Y, A and B" clauses.
- Hedge-then-assert pivots ("While the work is technical, it is also deeply human.").
- Topic sentences announcing a structural move ("Let me walk you through three reasons.").
- "Furthermore / Moreover / Indeed / Additionally / It's worth noting" — almost always droppable.
- Closing flourishes that add no information.
- Restating or mirroring the source prompt/JD anywhere — including openings that describe the role back to the reader ("The role asks for someone who can…"). Show evidence instead.

## Voice

- Specific over abstract. "Audited PFCG roles and SM20 logs" beats "managed enterprise security reviews."
- Active voice with named subjects. "I authored the brief" beats "The brief was authored."
- Concrete, source-backed numbers, with their exact current form and qualifier. Never drop or add a qualifier to make a number look better.
- **Evidence over capability.** Ban "I can", "I'm familiar with", "I'm comfortable with", "I'm drawn to". Carry every claim with a specific artifact, project, number, or outcome. No evidence: cut the claim, don't soften it.
- **Lead with proof.** Openings show the reader something they don't already have (a specific artifact, a named deliverable, a hard figure on a real system) — never intent or enthusiasm filler.
- One spelling convention, applied consistently (this system: American).
- Contractions fine in letters, not in resumes.

## Self-check before writing any file

1. Search the draft for "—"; rewrite any hit.
2. Search the banned-vocabulary list.
3. Count triads (more than one per 250 words: prune).
4. Read sentence openings aloud; vary consecutive same-shape openings.
5. Delete any closing sentence that adds no new information.
6. Search for hedge-qualifiers ("up to ") before figures; use the source's current exact form.
7. Search for content classes that belong in structured fields only (in this system: work-authorization details); strip from prose.
